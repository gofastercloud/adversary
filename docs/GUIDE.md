# Player guide

## The loop in one minute

1. Pick a **scenario** (an organisation and sector), a **doctrine** (your starting deck and hero power) and an
   **assurance level** (Training, ML1, ML2, ML3).
2. Walk the **map**: battles, elites, a boss per act, shops, rest sites, events and whiteboard sessions.
3. In a **battle** you have 3 energy a turn and draw 5 cards. Deploy controls onto assets (they stay), play actions, enact
   policies (two Programme slots), and augment controls. Then end the turn.
4. Between battles pick one of three cards, buy relics, upgrade card maturity (ML1 to ML3), remove clutter and answer a
   threat-modelling whiteboard.

## Reading a battle

- **Goal bar** (top right): what the adversary is trying to do and how far along it is. If it fills, you lose.
- **Intent**: the adversary's next move. Intel raises its fidelity (1 technique, 2 target, 3 power and STRIDE class).
  *Signature*, *baseline tradecraft*, *goal payoff* and *assessed intent* are labelled so you know where each came from.
- **Footholds** are hidden until a monitor, a scan or a hunt reveals them. Detection strength beats a foothold's stealth.
  Revealed footholds can be evicted by reducing their grip to zero. Undetected footholds are what goals like
  pre-positioning count.
- **Wards** per STRIDE class sit on each asset. When an attack lands or is blocked the event log shows **why**: which
  control, which mitigation or CTID mapping matched, which augment countered the technique.
- **Exposure** rises when the adversary is blocked or revealed; at the limit its operation is burned and you win.
- **Resilience** is your hit points. Losing a crown jewel or reaching zero ends the run.

## Specialising and deck building

Each doctrine leans on two CSF functions (Architect: protect and identify; Hunter: detect and respond; Resilience Engineer:
recover and protect; GRC Lead: govern and identify; Incident Commander: respond and recover). Rewards weight cards you can use,
but real mitigations matter more: before a fight, the briefing shows which ATT&CK mitigations blunt that adversary and
whether your deck covers them. Augments are the multipliers (FIDO2 on MFA, post-quantum crypto on encryption, signed logic on
a PLC): they only fit specific controls and counter specific techniques. **Consumables** are one-use expert plays (security
retainer, disable system, restore from backup); decide when they are worth spending.

## Whiteboard sessions

You are shown a data-flow diagram and scenarios. Say which STRIDE category applies. Elements can be spoofed, tampered with,
repudiated, disclosed, denied or elevated; **flows** can only be tampered with, disclosed or denied. Each answer comes with an
explanation and links.

## Tabletop exercises (TTX)

From the title screen. A facilitator-style run with timed injects and decisions rated best/ok/poor, each with a lesson and
references, scored against objectives. Use it in a workshop: project it, discuss each decision, then reveal the lesson.

## Progress

Clearance XP, a codex that fills as you meet adversaries and techniques (every entry links to the source), 122 achievements
(20 hidden), and a daily challenge with a verified leaderboard (every score is recomputed by replaying your actions).

## Tutorial walkthrough

The guided first battle is scripted so every message is true: it teaches deploying a ward, why a card shows its STRIDE
class, how Identify raises intent fidelity, how a hidden foothold is found and evicted, and what ends a battle. Replay it any time
from the title screen. The Guide screen lists every concept with its sources.
